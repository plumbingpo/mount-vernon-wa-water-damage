Dealing With Roof Leak Water Damage in Mount Vernon

The spot on your ceiling is seldom directly below the hole in the roof. A loose shingle, a failed flashing joint or an aging seal around a skylight lets rain in, and it travels across the roof deck and framing until it falls into the insulation. A ceiling stain tends to arrive late, well after the space overhead first got wet. Locating all of the moisture and drying it is our job.

We are a water damage crew, not a roofing company. We work on the interior, which means removing wet insulation, drying drywall and framing, dealing with mold and rebuilding the ceiling. When the roof is still letting water through, a temporary tarp is something we can install, and we will coordinate with your roofer so the repair comes before the ceiling is closed.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsWater coming through a ceiling fan, a light or close to an outlet means that circuit should be turned off at the panel, and the fixture should not be touched.
  2. Contain the WaterSet buckets or pans under active drips and move furniture, rugs and electronics out of the way. Protect the floor with plastic sheeting or old towels.
  3. Relieve a Ceiling BulgeWhen water pools in a bubble overhead, pierce the low point with a screwdriver while standing clear, and let it run into a bucket.
  4. Stay Off the RoofA wet roof and an unlit attic are both places to stay out of. Just observe the entry point from the room below, then hand the job to a trained crew.
  5. Photograph the DamageUse your phone to record the damaged ceiling, attic and belongings first, hold off on throwing things out until that is done, and call us to get the drying started.

Coverage

What About Insurance?

Homeowners policies often cover interior water damage when a roof leak is sudden and accidental, such as wind tearing off shingles or a limb puncturing the roof. Insurers generally turn down claims tied to wear, age or lack of upkeep, and they may handle the roof repair on different terms than the inside damage. Our team gives your adjuster photos and moisture readings, yet what is covered comes down to your policy, so check it or talk to your agent.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • A vent pipe boot whose rubber has split from long sun exposure.
  • Nails that have backed out of the decking and left small holes through the shingles.
  • Leaves and grit collecting where two roof slopes meet, damming rain until it seeps through.
  • Gaps at chimneys, vents and sidewalls where the flashing has separated or rusted through.
  • A skylight whose curb or seal has dried and cracked, so rain gets past it.

Where the Damage Spreads

Drywall Ceilings

Drywall that stays wet stains, sags and loses its strength. Seams split, the tape lets go, and a heavy enough section can drop with little warning.

Insulation in the Attic

Once soaked, insulation flattens, loses its value and keeps the drywall under it damp for days. Fiberglass can sometimes be dried. Cellulose that is saturated generally comes out.

Rafters and Decking

Wood in the roof structure that is soaked over and over darkens, swells and softens. Once they rot, the frame supporting the roof is weaker.

Mold in the Attic

Mold does well in an attic that is warm, dark and full of damp wood and drywall paper. Within 24 to 48 hours it can start growing, then move across the bottom of the roof deck.
