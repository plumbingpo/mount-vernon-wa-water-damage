Floods start in many ways: heavy rain, storm surge, a river over its banks, a dead sump pump, a sewer backup or a major plumbing failure, and all of them call for fast professional help. Expect gray water (Category 2) or black water (Category 3) in almost any flood, and with it bacteria, contamination and potentially hazardous substances that threaten your health. Mount Vernon Restoration handles emergency flood cleanup across Mount Vernon and answers 24/7 to pump out the water, sanitize what it touched and restore your property safely.

The first job is getting the water out quickly, and our submersible and truck-mounted pumps are able to clear thousands of gallons every hour. Next our technicians, who hold IICRC certification, judge the level of contamination and choose a sanitizing plan that covers HEPA air filtration, antimicrobial application and disinfecting each surface that got wet. We tear out and properly dispose of what cannot be saved, such as soaked carpet padding, contaminated drywall and ruined insulation.

Once the space is extracted and sanitized, our team brings in commercial drying gear, namely low-grain refrigerant (LGR) dehumidifiers plus high-velocity air movers, so the structural components dry all the way through. Daily readings from calibrated moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera tell us when everything is fully dry, which is what keeps mold from growing. Every stage of the job is recorded for your claim: photographs, detailed moisture maps, logs of the equipment used and the scope-of-work reports that insurance adjusters require.

Warning Signs for Flood Cleanup

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Tide marks on drywall or trim where the water peaked
  • Appliances such as a water heater, washer or dishwasher spilling water across the floor
  • Any amount of water sitting in a room, garage or basement
  • A layer of silt, dirt or debris on the floor after water recedes
  • Ground so saturated that puddles sit against the foundation walls
  • Seepage at door thresholds, window wells or cracks in the foundation
  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or drywall that feels soft and wet

The Benefits of Professional Flood Cleanup

Getting the standing water out is only one part of cleaning up after a flood. The water brings sewage, chemicals, bacteria and more with it, and all of that is a serious hazard to health. Professional flood crews are trained in containment, personal protective equipment (PPE) and sanitizing protocols that keep your family from exposure to pathogens such as E. coli, hepatitis and other waterborne diseases.

Beyond the health concerns, a poor flood cleanup often leaves hidden moisture that turns into severe mold growth within days. Our technicians rely on psychrometric calculations, meter readings and thermal imaging to verify that each affected spot, from the framing to the subfloor to the inside of the walls, meets accepted drying standards. Nothing else reliably heads off secondary damage, and that damage can run thousands more to remediate, so we dry by the numbers.

How to Prevent Flood Cleanup Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Look into a flood insurance policy, including when your home sits outside a mapped flood zone
  • Store valuables and important papers off the basement floor in waterproof containers
  • Keep gutters and storm drains clear of debris so water can flow properly
  • Install backwater valves on sewer lines to stop sewage from backing up into your home
  • Cracks in the foundation should be sealed and the basement walls given a waterproof coating
  • Make sure the ground slopes away from your foundation on every side
